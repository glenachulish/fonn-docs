# Privacy Policy — Fonn

*Last updated: 5 November 2026*

Fonn collects nothing.

That is the whole policy, but the App Store requires it spelled out, and a
one-line answer to a serious question deserves the detail behind it.

## What the app stores, and where

Your tunes, settings of them, sets, collections, notes, ratings, tags, links,
practice tempos and practice loops are kept in a database inside the app's own container on your
device. Recordings you make and files you import — audio, video, PDFs, photos
of sheet music — are kept as files in that same container.

There is no Fonn account, no Fonn server and no Fonn sign-in, and there never
will be. Nobody, including the developer, can see your library.

If you switch on iCloud sync in Settings, your library is also kept in **your
own iCloud account**, so the same tunes are on your iPhone, your iPad and your
Mac. It goes into your account's private database. Apple encrypts it, the
developer has no access to it and no way to ask for access, and no other user
of Fonn can reach it. The syncing is between your devices and your iCloud;
nobody else is party to it. Switch it off and the copy on the device stays,
and simply stops syncing.

If you have iCloud Backup switched on, your device may also include the app's
data in your own encrypted backup. Both that and the sync above are Apple's
services under your own Apple Account, covered by Apple's privacy policy rather
than this one.

## What the app does not do

- No analytics, telemetry or crash reporting of any kind.
- No advertising, and no advertising identifier.
- No tracking. Nothing goes to any other company unless you ask for it, in the
  ways described below.
- No Fonn accounts, and Fonn does not collect your email address, name or
  location.
- No third-party SDKs.

## When Fonn does use the internet

Fonn works with no signal at all, and most of what it does never touches the
network. But it is a tool for finding and hearing tunes as well as keeping them,
so some things do reach out. Each happens only because you asked for it.

**Searching thesession.org.** When you search or open a tune there, your device
contacts thesession.org exactly as a browser would. Nothing identifying you or
Fonn is sent — no account, no identifier — and only your search terms and the
tune you chose travel. What that site logs is governed by their privacy policy,
not this one.

**Finding tunes elsewhere.** When you add a tune from a website or the FluteFling
catalogue, your device contacts the site you chose: abcnotation.com,
tunearch.org, nigelgatherer.com, norbeck.nu, flutefling.scot, or any address you
type. Importing from a YouTube link asks YouTube for the video's title. Where a
site has no search of its own, and when you search the whole web or Spotify,
your search words go to DuckDuckGo. Searching Bandcamp sends them to Bandcamp.
What those services log is governed by their own policies.

**Apple Music.** If you search Apple Music or play an Apple Music link, Fonn uses
Apple's MusicKit, and your Apple device asks your permission the first time. Your
searches and what you play go to Apple under your own Apple Account, as they
would in the Music app, and are covered by Apple's privacy policy. Fonn keeps only
what you chose to attach: the link, its name and any practice loop. You can
withdraw permission in Settings, under Privacy & Security → Media & Apple Music.

**Spotify and Bandcamp.** When you play one of these links under the music, Fonn
shows that service's own player, so your device contacts the service as your
browser would. For Bandcamp, Fonn also reads the public page you linked to, to
find the right player. These services may set cookies, which stay in Fonn's web
storage on your device.

If you choose **Sign in to Spotify**, you type your details into Spotify's own
page. Fonn does not read or keep your password, and the developer receives
nothing. Spotify's sign-in stays in Fonn's web storage on your device, and Spotify
can see what you play. **Sign out of Spotify**, on the player's menu or in Fonn's
Settings, removes it from that device. What Spotify and Bandcamp collect is theirs
to describe, not mine.

**Fetching instrument sounds.** Four instruments are carried inside the app so
it plays with no signal. The rest are downloaded once, from
`paulrosen.github.io`, the first time you choose them, and kept on your device
afterwards. You can delete them again in Settings.

**Opening a web page you asked for.** Fonn can show a page — a tune archive, a
YouTube or Vimeo video attached to a tune — in a web view inside the app. At that moment
your device contacts that site as your browser would, and what they collect is
theirs to describe, not mine. Nothing about Fonn or you is added to the
request.

A tune you already have, played from its notation or your own recordings,
contacts nothing, apart from iCloud fetching a recording if you use iCloud sync.

## The microphone

If you record yourself playing, or use the tuner, iOS asks your permission for
the microphone the first time. A recording is written straight to a file in the
app's own container. The tuner listens and shows you a pitch, and keeps
nothing. Neither is transmitted, processed elsewhere, or transcribed.

You can withdraw the permission at any time in Settings, under Privacy &
Security → Microphone, or under Fonn's own entry in Settings.

## The camera and your photos

Photographing printed music uses the camera, and attaching an existing picture
uses your photo library — both only when you choose them, and both stay in the
app's own container. Photographs are never uploaded.

Reading the notes off a photograph happens on your device, using Apple's own
text recognition. The image does not leave the phone to be read.

## Files you share out

Exporting a tune or a whole library uses the standard share sheet, so the file
goes wherever you choose to send it and nowhere else. A shared Fonn file carries
the tune or set, its notes, its links with their names and practice loops, and
its PDFs. It does not carry your recordings, videos or photos.

Fonn's own storage is not browsable from the Files app. Everything comes out
through the app — export a library, or share a single recording — which is
also what keeps the library consistent: files there are named for the database
rather than for you, and deleting one by hand would leave a tune pointing at
something that is no longer there.

## Buying the full version

Fonn is free to download and lets you add up to 20 tunes. A single one-off
in-app purchase removes that limit. The purchase is made and processed
entirely by Apple, through the App Store. Fonn never sees your payment details,
and the developer receives no personal information about who bought it. The app
only asks Apple whether this Apple Account has bought the full version, and
remembers the answer on your device. It also keeps a count of how many tunes
you have added, on your device and in your own iCloud account (including iCloud
Keychain), so that the limit survives a reinstall. That count is a number and
nothing else.

## Children

Fonn has no accounts, no messaging, no user-generated content visible to anyone
else, and no advertising. The one purchase is made through Apple's App Store, as
above. Fonn collects nothing from anyone of any age.

## Changes

If this ever stops being true, the policy will be updated before the change
ships, and the date at the top will say when.
